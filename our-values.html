Our values

The rules we hold ourselves to.

Every company has values on a wall. Ours are written for the moments when doing the right thing costs us something: a later release, a smaller deal, a harder conversation. Each one says what it means in practice, so you can hold us to it.

01

Value one

Rigour you can audit.

If a number cannot be tested, it does not ship.

A client plan is a promise made in numbers, so we treat the engine behind it the way banks treat their core systems. Changes are proven before they reach you, and any result can be traced back to the exact model that produced it.

In practice

  • A fixed set of reference households is re-run on every change. If their results move and we cannot say why, the change does not go out.
  • Wherever the mathematics has an exact answer, the engine is checked against it.
  • Every result carries the version of the model that produced it, so last year's figure is never mistaken for today's.
  • Before major releases we run adversarial reviews whose only job is to break our own numbers.

What we will not do

  • Change how the model works without a new version number.
  • Wave through a result that moved for a reason nobody can explain.
02

Value two

Care in the small things.

The errors that hurt clients are rarely big. They are small, plausible and silent.

A date read as an amount. A credit limit read as a balance. A debit counted as a credit. Each looks harmless on its own, and each can put a wrong figure into a family's plan without anyone noticing.

We hunt these down one at a time, and we do not consider one fixed until there is proof it cannot come back.

In practice

  • Every defect we confirm gets its own permanent test, so it cannot quietly return.
  • A figure that is correct but incomplete arrives with a warning. A figure that would be wrong is held back for you to decide.
  • Negative amounts are understood however a bank writes them: a minus sign, a trailing minus, brackets or DR.

What we will not do

  • Round off a doubt to make a screen look complete.
  • Call a fix finished before a test proves it.
03

Value three

Candour.

A clear "not yet" beats a vague "sort of".

We would rather lose a deal than win it on a misunderstanding. Ask whether the software can do something and you get a straight answer. If the answer is no, you hear it from us before you discover it yourself.

In practice

  • When the platform cannot confirm that another system received something, it reports the outcome as unknown, never as a success.
  • Our known limitations are written down, and we walk you through them before you sign.
  • When we get something wrong, you hear it from us first, together with what we are doing about it.

What we will not do

  • Promise a delivery date we cannot keep.
  • Let a sales conversation get ahead of the software.
04

Value four

Collect less, keep less, send less.

The safest client data is the data that never moves.

Every copy of a client's information is a risk someone has to carry. So each feature is designed to touch as little as it can, and to fail closed, refusing to work rather than working unsafely, when something it needs is missing.

In practice

  • Credentials for custodian and CRM feeds are encrypted with a key kept apart from the database. Without that key, the platform refuses to store them at all.
  • A stored credential is never sent back to a browser, not even to the person who entered it.
  • An order sent to your own systems carries the account, the instrument and the amount. Who the client is travels only when you choose to include it.
  • Status messages and logs never repeat account numbers, client names or credentials.

What we will not do

  • Keep a secret in plain text because it is more convenient.
  • Collect a field just in case it is useful later.
05

Value five

Your work stays yours.

You should stay because the product is good, not because leaving is hard.

The plans in the platform hold years of your advisers' work with their clients. That work belongs to your firm, in a form you can carry with you.

In practice

  • Every plan can be exported to a file and imported again.
  • Files saved with an older version open in a newer one: they are upgraded automatically, then checked.
  • The database underneath is standard PostgreSQL, not a proprietary store.

What we will not do

  • Make your own data hard to take with you.
  • Lock a plan into a format only we can read.
06

Value six

Protect the record.

A client's plan is a record. Treat it like one.

Advice is judged later, sometimes years later. The plan behind it has to survive imports, upgrades and corrections without losing what was there before.

In practice

  • An import adds and updates. It never deletes anything from a plan.
  • Every applied import can be reversed with a single undo, back to the plan exactly as it was.
  • A file that fails its checks is refused, and the plan you had stays untouched.
  • Once an order has been sent, its record can no longer be altered or deleted.

What we will not do

  • Overwrite a client's plan silently.
  • Let anyone rewrite history after the fact.

When values collide

Good things sometimes pull in different directions. This is what wins.

Values only mean something when they decide a hard choice. These are the ones we settled in advance.

  • SpeedorcertaintyCertaintyA release can wait. A wrong number in a client's plan cannot be taken back.
  • A bigger dealora straight answerThe straight answerEvery time, even when the honest answer is "not yet".
  • Convenienceorless dataLess dataEven when it means one more step for you, or for us.
  • A clever featureora dependable oneDependableClever can come next. Dependable has to come first.

Hold us to it. If you ever find us falling short of one of these values, tell us. We treat it as a defect, not a difference of opinion.
