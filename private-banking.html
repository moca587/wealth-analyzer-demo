The challenge

The biggest decisions your clients face rarely sit in the portfolio.

01

The pension fund outweighs the portfolio

When to draw it, as capital or as an annuity, and in how many steps: for many households this is the largest financial decision of their lives, and it is often made without the bank.

02

Lending lives in another system

The mortgage is priced by one team and the portfolio advised by another. Repayments, interest and the value of the home rarely meet in one projection.

03

Advice that depends on who you get

Plan quality varies from one banker to the next. A client meeting should never be a lottery for your brand.

04

Data your bank already holds, typed in twice

Custody positions and client records already exist in your systems, yet they reach planning tools by hand, and the typing errors travel with them.

From your systems to the plan

Custody and CRM data in the plan, with a banker at every step.

A direct connection pulls positions and client records from your custodian and your CRM. Nothing is written until a banker has looked at it.

1

Connect

A connection your security team can sign off.

Point a connection at the custodian's statement feed or at your CRM's client records.

  • Requests to private or internal network addresses are refused
  • Connections can be pinned to a list of named hosts
2

Preview

A test fetch that writes nothing.

See exactly what the source sends, already translated into the plan's terms, before anything is applied.

3

Review

Each record matched to the right row.

Every record is sorted into new, changed or unchanged and grouped by section, with the old and the new value side by side.

  • Matched on what a record is, not only its name: a cash account named after a pension fund can never overwrite the pension
  • A spouse's salary arriving in the same file as the spouse lands with the right person
  • A row repeated within one file is recognised, not added again
4

Apply

Only what the source actually sent.

If a feed sends a loan balance without its rate, the rate already in the plan is left alone. Running the same statement a second time changes nothing at all, so a weekly refresh never grows the plan.

Across your bank

One platform, four seats at the table.

Your bankers use it in front of clients. Your desk heads, CIO and compliance team each get what they need from the same plan.

For the private banker

Lending and advice in one projection.

Mortgages sit in the plan as they stand on your books. Each one amortises year by year, with interest and principal paid out of the household's cash flow, so a repayment shows up as what it really costs.

  • Debt service on its own line in the cash-flow table
  • Spending entered without loan repayments, so nothing is counted twice
  • The home's value grows with its own uncertainty, around 3% a year

For the head of the desk

The same plan, whoever the client sees.

One method and one report across the desk. A newer banker hands over a plan that reads like a senior colleague's, so the client's experience no longer depends on the name on the door.

  • One planning method on every desk
  • Roles that decide who can see and change each plan
  • Per-banker access to client plans

For the CIO

One set of risk settings, applied to every plan.

Each client's risk profile and time horizon decide the return and volatility their plan runs on, so your house settings reach every banker in exactly the same way.

  • Seven risk profiles, from very conservative to very aggressive
  • A couple's two profiles blended into one
  • Time horizon weighed alongside appetite for risk

For compliance and risk

Your supervisor's questions, answered in advance.

The documents a vendor review asks for are ready before you ask, and every change to a plan shows who made it, and when.

  • Sign-in and an audit trail of changes to each plan
  • Security review and data processing agreement on request
  • Outsourcing terms written for supervised banks

Swiss depth

Swiss detail, down to the municipality.

Your Swiss clients rarely ask about markets alone. They ask when to draw the pension fund, how to spread the 3a accounts, and what their commune will take. Wealth Analyzer models each answer with official tax data.

  • The 13th AHV pension, alongside Pillar 2 and 3a
  • Capital-withdrawal tax on Pillar 2 and 3a, by canton and commune
  • Staggered withdrawals compared with a single one
  • Annuity or lump sum, with the age at which the annuity pays more

Where it pays off

Three moments a plan turns into business.

Pension decisions become mandates

When a client takes the capital, it needs a new home. The bank that modelled the choice with them is the natural one.

Mortgage renewals become reviews

A renewal brings the client to the table anyway. With the loan already in the plan, it turns into a conversation about everything else too.

A desk that grows into complexity

Shared method and settings let newer bankers take on more demanding households, sooner, without the senior team rewriting their work.
